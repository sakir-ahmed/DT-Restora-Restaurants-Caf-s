## 4. Offers and honest urgency

- **Core offer:** 1,000 BDT per month, 2,500 BDT installation, first month free, yearly plan with 2 months free.
- **Message that closes:** "Your first month is free and the installation fee is refundable after a year. The only thing you risk is the time to try it."
- **Urgency, only if true:** choose a real end date for the launch price (for example day 90) and say it plainly. Never invent a fake countdown or "only 3 seats left".
- **Yearly plan:** offer it at the close: "Pay ten months, use twelve." Aim for about 30% of customers.
- **Starter path:** for hesitant owners, begin with POS and kitchen only, then add stock, staff and the website in month two.

## 7. Sales process

### Lead handling
1. Reply within 15 minutes (business hours) with a short greeting and one question: "How many branches do you run, and do you use a billing system today?"
2. Offer two demo times, 20 minutes, by phone, video or in person.
3. Send the demo link and one relevant post (for example the profit dashboard).

### The 20-minute demo
1. 2 min: their situation and biggest headache.
2. 5 min: owner dashboard and profit build-up.
3. 5 min: POS split payment and kitchen display.
4. 4 min: stock, recipes and waste, plus staff attendance and salary.
5. 2 min: website and loyalty, only if they asked.
6. 2 min: offer, installation date and the next step.

### Follow-up cadence
Day 0 thank-you and recap, day 1 one benefit message, day 3 a short video or customer story, day 7 a gentle check-in with the offer, day 14 last check-in, then monthly.

### Objection handling

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "I am not good with computers" | Each role sees one simple screen, and there are Bangla guides. We train your cashier in a short session. |
| "What if the internet goes down?" | The POS and kitchen screen keep working offline. Cash orders sync later. |
| "It costs too much" | 1,000 BDT per month is about 33 BDT a day, the first month is free and the installation fee is refundable after a year. |
| "I already have a billing app" | Does it track recipe-level stock, payroll and online ordering? Keep your billing app until you see the demo, then decide. |
| "I will think about it" | Ask what exactly they need to see. Offer the live demo account to try with their team. |
| "My staff will resist" | Start with POS and kitchen only. Add modules once the team is comfortable. |

### Onboarding checklist (protects the sale)
Installation date set, menu and prices loaded, recipes entered for the top 20 dishes, staff roles created, cashier trained, receipt printer tested, first live day supported, day-7 review call, day-25 review before the first bill.

## 9. Tracking and weekly review

Use `lead-tracker-template.csv` (columns for source, post, status and dates) in Google Sheets and review it every Sunday.

| KPI | Weekly check |
|---|---|
| Leads, by source and by post tag | up or down against last week |
| Demos booked and held | 26 per week at full speed |
| Demo-to-sale rate | compare with 30% base case |
| Cost per lead and per demo (paid) | stop what is clearly worse |
| Installed and live customers | cumulative target in section 2 |
| Referrals asked and received | at least 2 asks per new customer |

**Decision rules:** if leads are low, add outreach and ads; if leads are fine but demos are low, speed up replies; if demos are fine but sales are low, rework the demo and the follow-up; if sales are fine but customers go quiet after installation, strengthen onboarding.

## 11. Risks and how to handle them

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Not enough leads | Increase direct outreach first, because it is free and fast |
| Slow replies | Assign one person to messages each shift |
| Installation backlog | Fix a weekly install capacity and schedule dates at the demo |
| Customers not adopting | Run the day-7 and day-25 reviews and keep the first scope small |
| Over-promising | Describe only what the system does today; the demo is the proof |
